## 1.3.0

### Minor Changes

- [#339](https://github.com/bielu/Bielu.AspNetCore.ApiDescriptions/pull/339) [`328f0b5`](https://github.com/bielu/Bielu.AspNetCore.ApiDescriptions/commit/328f0b5da0346364cb12524e083c7db5cc9eddc8) Thanks [@bielu](https://github.com/bielu)! - The Arazzo Sequence view can draw the calls services make to each other. Arazzo has no field for who makes a step's call (its runner makes them all), so a new opt-in option, `ActorExtension` (`actorExtension` in npm and on the plugin URL), names a specification extension such as `x-actor` on a step or a workflow. Its value is a source description's name, which puts the call on that source's lane, or any other name, which gets a lane of its own. The step's request, message or workflow call then starts from that lane, and the response or delivered message goes back to it. A lane calling itself is drawn as a loop, and the client's lane is left out when no step is the client's. `ScalarArazzoOptions.ActorExtension` and `ScalarArazzoAspireOptions.ActorExtension` are new.

  A second option, `ActorsExtension` (`actorsExtension`), names a document-level actor registry such as `x-actors`: actor name → `{ title, description, client }`. The lanes it lists are titled after it, with the description in their tooltip, and come first in its order; the entry with `client: true` renames the client's lane, and its steps are the client's. `ScalarArazzoOptions.ActorsExtension` and `ScalarArazzoAspireOptions.ActorsExtension` are new.

- [#341](https://github.com/bielu/Bielu.AspNetCore.ApiDescriptions/pull/341) [`762133f`](https://github.com/bielu/Bielu.AspNetCore.ApiDescriptions/commit/762133f68fa68389f8118977fba62d9bec4ce978) Thanks [@bielu](https://github.com/bielu)! - The Arazzo Sequence view draws the ways a step can end as a UML `alt` fragment instead of noting its failure paths under the arrow: the success path first (`[201]` and its response), then each conditional success action and each reachable failure action under its condition (`[400]`, `[if …]`, `[otherwise]`), with the response or message it ends on and where it leads (`rejected: end`, `→ create`, `retry after 2 s × 3`). The request stays above the `alt`, since every way starts with it.

Stages can be branches of a `par`, and a stage's own steps can run in parallel inside its band; nested frames are inset so every edge shows.

- [#338](https://github.com/bielu/Bielu.AspNetCore.ApiDescriptions/pull/338) [`bffa3db`](https://github.com/bielu/Bielu.AspNetCore.ApiDescriptions/commit/bffa3dbea6e74fc6d0c34d349c1d0689eeff7e50) Thanks [@bielu](https://github.com/bielu)! - The Arazzo Sequence view draws a step that invokes a workflow as a stage: the invoked workflow's own requests, responses and messages, in place, on a band numbered in run order and titled with the workflow's summary, instead of one arrow into a workflow lane.
